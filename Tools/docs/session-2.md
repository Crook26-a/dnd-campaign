# Session 2: The False Light

Session 2 takes the Sallowmay out of Kalatecha on the night of day 1, across two days of open sea (with merrow at dusk on day 2 and a long rest that night), through the reef by the false light, and ashore on Lantern Rock as far as the ship graveyard. It runs about 2 hours 25 minutes plus a 15-minute break. Wreckers' Cove, the lighthouse and the firebird are Session 3.

Tick each part as you finish it. The 📍 beside a part shows its place on the map. **▶ Set the scene** (in the run sheet, and under each part's heading) does the setting up in one click: where the party is, the time of day, where the ship is, and the right map or battle map, with tonight's enemies already placed. The "In the app" steps below are there if you'd rather do it by hand. Read-aloud text is in the shaded boxes; paraphrase it freely. If you're running long, play one beat on day 2 instead of two, and skip the Sentry's Perch: the cove simply isn't warned.

## Opening {10 min} {place: kship/sdeck}

Read the recap. It's Thimble's version of Session 1, perched on the chart table:

> "Right. For anyone who wasn't paying attention, which was most of you." Thimble clears her throat. "Four fools answered the captain's notice. The captain gave them jobs, which was generous of him. They went to the market, bought a lantern that burns without oil off a man with a thieving gull, and let a fellow in a monocle try to steal it out from under them."
> "Then Michael Fontaine at the Guild gave them a real job: chart a way through the reef at Lantern Rock, where the lighthouse shines all night and ships don't come back. Somewhere in there a little spirit pinched a dagger, and nobody even saw it happen." She glares at the empty air beside her, as if it might be there now.
> "Then six of the monocle man's thugs jumped them on the docks. It went badly for the thugs. Ander knocked them into the harbor with thunder, and then knocked a few more in by hand." A sniff. "And someone tried to keep the lantern in her bag. I took it back. It's on the chart table, where it belongs, and the captain says no mortal made the light inside it."
> "So. It's night. The tide is turning. And Lantern Rock is two days south. Any questions? Good."

Ask each player for one thing their character is doing as the ship gets ready, at night, on the dock. Then move on.

## Casting off {10 min} {place: kship/sdeck}

The Sallowmay leaves Kalatecha in the dark, half a day ahead of the Undaunted. Read:

> Nobody hauls a line. Nobody climbs the rigging. The mooring ropes slip their posts by themselves and coil on the deck like sleeping cats, and the sails, pale in the lamplight, fill with a wind none of you can feel on your faces. The Sallowmay slides out past the Admiralty's walls. Behind you, the city's lamps shrink to a string of gold beads. Ahead, the sea is black and full of stars.
> At the stern, Captain Callomay rests one hand on the rail. "Navigator. South, if you please."

**The captain sails her.** He sails the Sallowmay by magic, all day and all night, and nobody needs to work the ship. If anyone asks how, he says, "She knows the way once she's told it." That's the truth, and all of it he'll say.

**The navigation check (your table rule).** Xuna makes a Wisdom (Survival) check to set the course. Put the total in the voyage bar's **Nav** box (tick **nat 20** if it was one), then press **▶ Sail**. The bar works out the time:

- A natural 20 halves the voyage: about a day.
- A total of 10 or more: the plain sailing time, about 2 days and 3 hours. They reach the reef in the small hours of day 4.
- Under 10: half a day longer for each point below 10.

You can also log the roll in the Action box (Survival, DC 10) so it earns its XP.

If a bad roll would bring them in by daylight, the captain heaves to a few miles off and waits for dark: the false light only burns at night.

**The lantern.** On the chart table in the captain's quarters, the lantern's little flame leans south and a little east. It never wavers. A navigator who watches it all night and makes an Intelligence (Arcana) check, DC 12, notices it leans a hair further every hour: it isn't pointing at a place, it's pointing at something that moves a little. (DM only: the firebird shifting on its perch in the cage.)

## Day 2 at sea {15 min} {place: kship}

The ship stops by itself at 10 in the morning (the **Stop every** setting). Pick two of these, then **▶ Sail** again.

**The fish that won't die (the quartermaster, or whoever likes fishing).** Someone fishes off the stern for supper. For each catch, roll a d6: 1 to 3, a bad fish (a reef bully, an egg-thief); 4 to 6, a good one. A bad fish can be cleaned and cooked like any other. A good fish won't die: the hook slides out of its lip without a mark, the knife won't cut it, it flops about quite happily until someone throws it back. If they ask the captain, he answers truthfully: "Nothing that doesn't deserve it comes to harm on my ship." A player who makes an Intelligence (Arcana) check, DC 15, recognizes old fae guest-law. (See "No innocents aboard" in the Captain Callomay document. The crew aren't covered: "You're not my guests. You're my crew.")

**The bird with the hook (the medic).** A storm petrel drops onto the deck, a rusty fishhook through its wing. It hops straight past everyone to the captain's feet and dips its head to him, the way a sailor ducks to an admiral. (A clue: sea creatures and seabirds treat him with odd respect.) Taking the hook out cleanly is a Wisdom (Medicine) check, DC 12. If Rin saves the wing, the petrel circles the masts and stays with the ship. Remember it: at dusk it shrieks a warning before the merrow climb aboard, and on the reef it flies ahead over the safe water.

**Iron below decks (the master-at-arms).** Ander takes stock of the ship's weapons. The locker is all bronze: blades, buckles, even the rivets. Then there are the three heavy crossbows and two maces Ethal brought aboard from the docks: plain iron. When the captain passes them, he steps around them the way you'd step around a puddle, and says, mildly, "Keep those in the hold, would you? Somewhere I won't put my hand on them." Don't explain. (DM only: cold iron is the one thing he can't touch. It matters again at the lighthouse, where the firebird's cage is cold iron.) The crossbows will be handy at dusk.

**Something big (anyone on watch).** Around noon, far off the port side, three dark humps rise out of a calm sea in a long line, and sink again without a splash. A player who makes a Wisdom (Perception) check, DC 15, sees a long head lift for a moment, look straight at the ship, and duck away. The captain lifts one hand from the rail, the way you'd wave to a neighbor. "She's only curious." (It's the same kind of beast the players' chart draws in the empty water. Some of them may be real. Who can say.)

## Dusk on day 2: the merrow {25 min} {place: kship/sdeck}

As the sun goes down on day 2, two merrow come up the hull, each with a red cap on its head. (Your table rule, from the Irish stories: the red cap is what lets a merrow walk on land.) In the app, pause the voyage (⏸) when the clock reaches dusk, about 7 in the evening, or set the time of day to dusk by hand. Read:

> The sun sinks into the sea ahead of you and turns the water the color of wine. The wind is soft, the ship hums along, and someone is already thinking about supper.
> Then the hull booms, once, as if something heavy struck it from below. Then again, nearer the stern. And over the rail on the port side comes a hand, long and grey-green and barnacled, with fingers as thick as rope, and then a second, and then a head: wide, flat, all mouth, with eyes like black glass. A rope-wrapped harpoon of yellowed bone rises out of the water beside it.

(If Rin saved the petrel, it shrieks and dives at the rail a heartbeat before the first hand appears: no one is surprised.)

The captain doesn't move from the wheel. Read:

> Captain Callomay looks at the thing on his rail, and for once he isn't smiling. "Those aren't curious." He doesn't draw his sword. "Master-at-arms. The deck is yours."

He doesn't fight: boarders aren't guests, but he leaves the fighting to his crew. Thimble shouts advice from the rigging, most of it rude, some of it useful.

**In the app.** First bring everyone up on deck: Where tab → **All together**, then put the party on the Main deck (anyone below decks climbs up the ladder on their first turn). Enter the Sallowmay and open the [[place:kship/sdeck|Main deck]] (Enter this place); it opens calm. In the battle panel, **Bring someone in**: two [[foe:lf_redcap|red-capped merrow]] (in the Library under Foes), against the party. Put them at the port rail, one amidships and one by the stern. Then **Start the battle**. Light: dusk, so everything on deck is in Dim Light. Two merrow are 900 XP, a Moderate fight for four level-3 characters under the 2024 rules: harder than the docks, and they have a long rest tonight to recover, so let them spend everything.

**What the merrow do (SRD 5.2, plus the red caps).**

- Two attacks a turn, any mix of Bite, Claw and Harpoon. A bite leaves the target Poisoned until the end of the merrow's next turn.
- **The harpoon** hits from the water at up to 60 feet, and pulls a Large or smaller target up to 15 feet straight toward the merrow. A merrow at the rail can drag a character across the deck and over the side.
- **The red caps** let them come aboard and fight on deck at 30 feet (they swim 40). Knock or snatch a cap off with Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), or a Strength (Athletics) shove, against DC 13, and that merrow is back to the SRD's 10 feet on land: it lurches for the rail and the sea. That's the clever way to win this fight, so reward anyone who tries it. Ethal's quick hands are made for it.
- When one goes down, the other dives and is gone. They're hunters, not heroes.

**Over the side.** A character pulled into the sea is in Deep water: swimming costs each foot of movement 1 extra foot. Climbing back up the hull is a Strength (Athletics) check, DC 10, or no check at all if a crewmate throws them a rope (an action). In the water, a merrow gets the swimmer to itself, so the crew will want to be quick.

**Things to reward on deck.**

| Action | Check | Result |
| Cut a harpoon rope | An attack against AC 12, or Ethal's knife as a Bonus Action | The pull fails, and that merrow has no harpoon until it dives for another |
| Swing out on a line to reach the rail | Dexterity (Acrobatics), DC 10 | Land beside a merrow and get advantage on the next attack |
| Drop a spar or a water barrel on a climbing merrow | Strength (Athletics), DC 12 | It falls back into the sea and loses its next turn climbing |
| Fire the iron crossbows from the stern | — | Heavy crossbows from the docks, if they kept them |

**Afterward.** Press **End the fight** for the XP card. Any cap left behind is [[loot:ll_redcap|a merrow's red cap]]: damp however long it's out of the water, and whoever wears it can breathe underwater (your table rule; it's in the Library). Very handy in the flooded hold of the Silver Tern. In the merrow's net, tangled with weed, is a tin locket on a broken chain, engraved T.P., with a tiny painting of a girl inside. Hold on to it: the stowaway on the Heron knows it (it's their father's; he's one of the sailors held in Wreckers' Cove). (A suggestion: change the initials if you rename the stowaway.)

## Night 2 and day 3 {10 min} {place: kship}

**The long rest.** The crew sleeps; everyone gets the benefits of a Long Rest (Party tab: **Long rest · everyone**). The ship sails on through the night by herself.

**Supper with the captain (a chance at favor).** Before they sleep, the captain has the crew to supper in his quarters, the first time he's done it. The pork is very good. If anyone asks, Thimble says the butcher swore that pig was "mean as a snake: bit his boy twice." Thanking him for the meal or the ship, or asking politely about his story, earns favor at the end of the night. Rin (−4) and Ethal (−9) both have room to win some back here; keep it generous. If they ask about Lantern Rock, he says, "I've seen that light from a long way off, for a long time. I've never been closer than I mean to be tomorrow night." He won't say more, and he looks south as he says it.

**Sails astern.** At dawn on day 3, the master-at-arms (or whoever is on watch) spots sails far behind: the Undaunted, following the same course and slowly gaining. A DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check makes out the monocle glinting at her rail. The crew is still about half a day ahead.

The rest of day 3 is quiet. The lantern's flame burns brighter the farther south they go.

## The false light {20 min} {place: klantern}

The Sallowmay reaches the reef in the dark, and the lighthouse blazes up. In the app, the voyage ends by itself when she arrives; set the time to Night if it isn't already. Read:

> The wind drops. The sea goes smooth and black as oil. Somewhere ahead, white water whispers over rock you can't see. And then, without a spark or a flicker, a light opens on the dark, high on a crag ahead: a great warm gold, brighter than any lamp, so bright it throws your shadows long across the deck behind you.
> It is beautiful. It is welcoming. It is exactly where a ship would steer for safe harbor. And somewhere in the dark below it, the sea is breaking on teeth.
> On the chart table, the little lantern's flame has turned to point straight at it, and it's burning twice as bright.

**The navigator's choice.** Following the light runs the ship straight onto the reef; that's what the wreckers built it for. The stars are the honest guide. Xuna makes a Wisdom (Survival) check, DC 15, to steer by the stars through the one safe channel. Help the table make it a crew effort:

- The petrel (if Rin saved it) flies ahead over the safe water: advantage.
- A Circle of the Stars druid knows these stars better than anyone; if Xuna uses Starry Form or reads the sky aloud, let it count.
- Rin, Ethal or Ander can call out breakers in the dark: Wisdom (Perception), DC 12. Each success adds +2 to Xuna's total. Elves and the drow see the white water first.
- The captain won't steer for them. If asked, he says, "Your choice, Navigator. It always is." (The bargain: only charts made by mortal choice count.)

**On a success,** the Sallowmay slips between two black reefs with a hand's breadth to spare and glides into the sheltered water off the east side. Rebekah draws the channel on the crew's map: the first safe chart through the reef, which is half of what the Guild is paying for.

**On a failure,** the hull grinds over rock with a long shudder. Everyone makes a Dexterity saving throw, DC 12, or takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage from being thrown across the deck. The ship limps into the same safe water, scraped and leaking, not wrecked. By morning the scrape has closed over, like bark over a cut. (A clue: the Sallowmay is grown, not built: willow and hawthorn, and she heals. Mention it once, at dawn, if anyone looks.)

**Reveal the reef.** In the Players' screen panel, brush the reef and the channel into view on the players' chart, or un-hide the reef shapes in their own panels.

## Snack break {15 min}

Good stopping point: they're through the reef, the light is burning above them, and the island is right there.

## Ashore {10 min} {place: klantern/lland}

The captain stays aboard, as always. Read:

> The captain lowers the boat himself and holds it against the hull while you climb down. "I'll be here," he says. "I always am." Thimble hesitates on the rail, then folds her wings and stays with him. "Somebody has to keep an eye on him," she says, which is not what her face says.
> You row toward a steep bank of shingle on the east side, the one place on the island a boat can come ashore. The stones crunch under the keel. Above you, bare rock climbs toward the lighthouse, and its light sweeps out over your heads, never down at you. Around the shoulder of rock to the west, in a lagoon between two stony arms, the dark shapes of ships lie tilted in the water, masts leaning like broken fingers.

**Go ashore in the app.** On the sea chart, press **⚓ Go ashore** (or open Lantern Rock and move the party to [[place:klantern/lland|the Shingle Landing]]). Its map is a beach. It's night: the drow and the elves see in the dark; Ander needs a lantern or a torch (there are lanterns aboard, and the ship has plenty of oil, oddly).

**Where they can go.** Show them the island from the landing: the cliff path up to the lighthouse; the lagoon of wrecks to the west. Let them choose. If they head straight for the lighthouse, the wreckers on the stair are the start of Session 3: let them reach the tower door and end the night there instead.

## The ship graveyard {35 min} {place: klantern/ltern}

A sheltered lagoon full of wrecks: three lost this year, older hulks under them, green with weed. Read:

> The lagoon is still and smells of salt and rot. Three ships lie in it, newer than the weed-black ribs around them: a broad merchantman broken across the reef at the mouth, her middle stove in; a smaller ship beached on her side at the head of the lagoon; and a third, picked clean, lying low in the shallows. The lighthouse beam passes over all of them and never lands on any.

**The Sentry's Perch (hidden).** On the west arm of the lagoon, under a scrap of sailcloth, a wrecker sits with a brass bell on a spar, watching the reef. If he rings it, Wreckers' Cove is warned for Session 3: the wreckers are ready, and the captured sailors are moved deeper in. Each character who comes near makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check against his passive Perception (it's on his sheet in the app). If they're spotted, he reaches for the bell: anyone within reach can stop him first. Ethal can silence him without a sound. A captured sentry is a fine source of information if he's treated decently. In the app, **un-hide** [[place:klantern/lsentry|Sentry's Perch]] when they find it.

**The Silver Tern and the octopus.** The biggest wreck, broken-backed on the reef, her hold half-flooded. Salvage is still aboard, and a [[foe:srd:giant-octopus|giant octopus]] lives in the hold. Use [[place:klantern/ltern|the Wreck of the Silver Tern]] in the app: its battle map shows the tilted deck, the broken mainmast, and the flooded hold where the octopus waits. Enter it calm, and **Start the battle** when it grabs someone. For four level-3 characters this is a Low fight under the 2024 rules: one big monster, lots of reach, and a chance to be clever.

- **What it does (SRD 5.2):** its tentacles reach 10 feet, and a Medium or smaller creature they hit is Grappled (escape DC 13) and Restrained while it's held. Once a day, when it takes damage underwater, it can fill the water around it with ink and swim off (Ink Cloud).
- **Good moves to reward:** breaking a grabbed friend free; fighting from the deck rather than wading into the flooded hold; using the broken mainmast as a bridge (Dexterity (Acrobatics), DC 10).
- **When it's badly hurt,** let it use its ink and jet out to sea through the broken hull rather than fight to the death. It's an animal, not a villain.
- **Afterward:** press **End the fight** for the XP card. Then the salvage: Wisdom (Perception), DC 10, finds [[loot:ll_salvage|the salvage]] (brass fittings, a ship's bell, a waterlogged chest of cloth) and the coin.

**The Marigold.** Stripped bare. Gouges in her hull show where crates were pried loose. A character who makes an Intelligence (Investigation) check, DC 12, finds a scrap of stenciled board: MARIGOLD. (A clue for later: the same crates are down in the Undaunted's hold.)

**The Heron and the stowaway.** A small ship beached on her side. In her hold, behind a wall of broken barrels, the [[npc:ln_stowaway|young stowaway]] has been living on hardtack and rainwater for weeks. Read:

> Something moves behind the barrels. Then a voice, thin and fierce: "Stay back. I've got a knife." A face appears over the top, sunburned, hair in salty knots, holding up a knife that is very clearly a spoon.

They're terrified, half-starved, and sure everyone is a wrecker. Winning their trust is the medic's and the quartermaster's moment: food, water, a blanket, a kind word. Wisdom (Insight) or Charisma (Persuasion), DC 12, or no roll at all for a good gesture. Rin tending them counts (and healing them outside a fight earns her XP). Once they trust the crew, they talk fast:

- The wreckers are "the queen's people." Raven Heartgrove. (Her look is still yours to decide. A suggestion for the stowaway to say: "She has a red coat, and she laughs all the time.")
- They live in a sea cave under the north cliffs. They keep the sailors from the wrecks there, roped up, "for ransom, they said."
- **If the crew has the locket from the merrow:** the stowaway goes very still. "That's my da's. He was bosun on the Heron. They took him to the cave." Now they have a name to rescue.
- "There's a stair. Cut in the rock, from up by the lighthouse down into the cave. They think nobody knows." (The stowaway can show them the [[place:klantern|Wreckers' Stair]], so no Perception check is needed to find it. Un-hide it when they do.)
- "A man comes in a ship with a gold figurehead. He has a glass in one eye. He buys what they take." (Hargrove-Vance.)
- "There's a bird in the light. I hear it at night. It sings like it's crying."
- What they want most: to get off this island. Taking them to the Sallowmay is safe; the ship's law covers guests, and the stowaway is one.

## Cliffhanger {10 min} {place: klantern/llight}

End the night with the island waking up. Pick whichever fits what happened:

- **If the sentry rang the bell:** torches spill out of a crack in the north cliffs, voices shout, and the lighthouse beam swings down toward the lagoon for the first time all night.
- **If they got through unseen:** the lighthouse light flares white for a heartbeat, and from the top of the tower comes a long, high cry, like a bird. The stowaway grabs someone's sleeve. "That's it. That's the bird."
- **Either way,** as the sky greys toward dawn, there are sails on the horizon to the north: the Undaunted, an hour or two out, coming in on the safe channel as if she knew it all along.

Read:

> The light at the top of the tower burns on, and for the first time you can almost see the shape inside it: something with wings, beating against bars.

End the session there.
