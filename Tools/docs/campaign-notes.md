# Age of Exploration Campaign Notes

Oct 5, 2026 · @Joseph Crook

## Campaign overview

The crew sails out from Kalatecha, a bustling port city-state, to explore uncharted islands, mapping the unknown and bringing back treasure and stories. Each island is its own adventure; the city ties them together.

- **System:** Dungeons & Dragons, in an original setting built by the DM.
- **Players:** the DM's girlfriend (27), her brother (15), and her sisters (13 and 11). The three siblings have played about three sessions before; this is the DM's first campaign.
- **Tone:** swashbuckling adventure and mythical wonder. Islands are ruins, monster lairs, or home to peoples the crew trades and allies with — not lands to conquer.
- **Structure:** sail out, explore an island, come home to sell maps and resupply, repeat.
- **Table goals:** snappy combat, and every player gets a spotlight moment each session.

## The home port

Kalatecha is a sunny, walled harbor city-state where every voyage begins and ends. Tall-masted ships crowd the docks, a stone sea fortress guards the coast, and a great domed cathedral rises over a busy market square. Palm trees and terraced hills climb behind the red-tiled rooftops.

Show the players the city picture at the first session and let them point to where they want to go.

- **City name:** Kalatecha, an independent city-state.
- **City crest and flag:** set by the DM — the picture shows a real historical banner, so give the city its own.

## The main island

Kalatecha sits on the west coast of a large island, about 70 miles across and 125 miles from north to south. Everything beyond the city is hidden from the players for now. They can see only the first 5 miles of the southern road.

- **Who holds it:** the interior is its own country, with **Dunmarrow** on the north coast as its port. **Kalatecha** and **Saltmere** are free city-states. A possible plot point: two free cities trading past a country that surrounds them both.
- **Tolls:** each free city keeps a toll booth on every road out of it, with a hefty toll:
  - Kalatecha has three: Southgate Toll, Northgate Toll and Gullhaven Road Toll, each 10 miles out.
  - Saltmere has one: Saltmere Toll, 8 miles out.
  - **The toll (Joseph's table rule):** 1 gp a head and 5 gp a cart or wagon, each way, plus more on trade goods, at the DM's call.
- **Places:**
  - Cities: Dunmarrow (north) and Saltmere (south-east coast).
  - Towns: Briarford, Tallow Cross, Hollin's Rest, Greywater, Gullhaven and Thornwick.
  - Halfway House, a waystation.
  - Kalatecha Light, the lighthouse at the harbor mouth.
  - Nothing is inside any of them yet.
- **Land:** forest covers the island wherever there's no road, town or hill:
  - The Eastwold, Brackenmoor Wood, the Hollowheart, Gullhaven Wood, Thornwick Copse and Kingsfell Wood.
  - The Inland Hills sit in the middle.
  - The forest is hidden from the players too, because its gaps would show where the roads run.

## DM only: the captain's secret

The crew believes the ship's captain is a warlock with a fae patron. In truth, he is the fae, and his true name is Calandrous Callomaydare. How each player treats him quietly shapes their luck, and they never see the score.

### Favor track

Keep a hidden score for each player from −15 to +15, starting at 0 (Joseph's table rule, 2026-10-10). Move it whenever his mood toward them changes; how far is your call. The wide scale leaves room for small shifts in mood without the effect jumping.

| Raises favor | Lowers favor |
| Thanking him for the ship or his hospitality | Mocking or insulting him |
| Including him in plans and celebrations | Ignoring him or bossing him around |
| Bringing him a gift or a share of treasure | Breaking a promise to him |
| Keeping their word | Lying to him |
| Asking about his story politely | Prying into his true name |

**What it does.** Outside of battle only, and only when you decide it applies, add the bonus (or penalty) for their score to a roll:

| Score | On rolls |
| +11 to +15 | +3 |
| +6 to +10 | +2 |
| +1 to +5 | +1 |
| 0 | No effect |
| −1 to −5 | −1 |
| −6 to −10 | −2 |
| −11 to −15 | −3 |

The Party tab shows each player's score with its bonus beside it, for example "favor +7 (+2)".

### Clues to drop

- The ship never seems to need repairs, and no one ever sees him pay for supplies.
- He won't touch cold iron; his sword and the ship's fittings are bronze.
- He never gives his full name.
- Milk sours when he's annoyed, and flowers open when he's pleased.
- Sea creatures and seabirds that come near the ship treat him with odd respect.

### Running it

- Keep penalties mild and rewards generous, so the youngest player isn't punished for a joke.
- Let players notice the pattern on their own; never confirm it.
- Save the reveal for a big moment, such as when the crew stands up for him or badly betrays him.
- **Safety net:** if a character dies, especially the youngest player's, the captain appears out of thin air beside them and brings them back. It is the only time he ever leaves the ship, and that rescue doubles as the reveal. Use it only once, keep it open to any player, and consider a small fae price, like a favor owed or a silver mark on the skin.

## The crew and ship

The ship belongs to the captain, who hires the players as his crew: they run it and map the world from its deck.

- **Ship name:** the Sallowmay, the captain's ship.
- **Crew roles:** each player takes one of medic, navigator, quartermaster, or master-at-arms (the crew's fighter, who leads in battle and keeps the ship's weapons). The DM plays the ship's captain and patron, an NPC who owns the ship and hires the crew. He never leaves the ship and leaves the decisions to the crew. Roles give everyone a job and a reason to care about the ship.
- **The blank map:** hand the players graph or hex paper with only the home port drawn in. As they sail, they draw coastlines, name islands, and mark dangers.
- **Ship's log:** one player writes a few lines after each session; read it aloud to recap at the start of the next.
- **Sailing time:** the captain sails the Sallowmay by magic, 24 hours a day, with no crew needed. At a sailing ship's 2 mph that's about 48 miles a day, or half that against a strong wind.
- **Navigation check (table rule):** before each voyage the navigator makes a check. A natural 20 halves the voyage time; a total under 10 adds half a day for each point below 10.
- **New islands:** an island goes on the players' chart as a route when the crew gets a mission there. Anything else turns up when the ship comes within about 10 miles of it.

## Session 1 plan

Session 1 stays in the city so the DM can find their footing and the players warm up with low stakes, and ends with the ship leaving the harbor.

- **Meet the crew.** Each player introduces their character. They report aboard the captain's ship, already hired; he asks each a last interview question, then gives them their crew roles.
- **The market.** The crew explores the market square. A merchant has a strange item from an unknown island and won't say where it came from.
- **The rival.** A cocky rival explorer from the upper town shows up, wants the same item, and boasts about sailing first.
- **The job.** Michael Fontaine of the Guild of Explorers offers a commission: chart the island the item came from and bring back proof.
- **A small challenge.** A short scuffle on the docks or a chase through the market refreshes combat and ability checks.
- **Set sail.** End the night with the cathedral bells ringing as the ship leaves the harbor.

Aim for 2 to 3 hours with snack breaks. Give each player at least one moment where their choice changes what happens.

## The first voyage and beyond

The first island is an original one, built by the DM to fit the campaign's vibe and tied to the strange item from the market.

- **First island:** keep it small — a handful of locations, one or two fights, one mystery or puzzle, and a clear prize to bring home. It's the island the market item came from.
- **Coming home:** after each island, the crew returns to the home port to sell their map, heal, resupply, and pick up the next rumor.
- **Later islands:** Ghosts of Saltmarsh adds rules for ships, sailing, and naval combat, plus seafaring adventures to drop in as new islands or ports. It was written for the 2014 rules but works alongside the newer ones.
- **The rival explorer:** shows up every few islands, sometimes ahead of the crew, sometimes needing rescue. Gives the campaign a throughline without heavy plotting.
- **Island ideas to fill in:** ruins of a lost people, a monster's lair, a friendly island to trade with, a place that isn't on any map.

## First island: Lantern Rock

A lonely island whose lighthouse has shone every night for as long as anyone remembers, though no one lives there. Ships that follow its light don't come back. It should take 2 to 3 sessions.

**The secret.** The light is a firebird, a great flame-feathered bird locked in an iron cage at the top of the lighthouse. The cage is cold iron, the one thing the captain can't touch: he won't ask the crew to free it, but he'll be very glad if they do. Wreckers led by the pirate queen Raven Heartgrove use its light to lure ships onto the reef, then loot the wrecks. The rival explorer buys their loot, and now wants the firebird itself.

**The hook.** The merchant's strange item is a lantern that burns without oil, salvaged from a wreck on the reef. Three ships have vanished near Lantern Rock this year. Michael Fontaine hires the crew to chart a safe channel through the reef and find out what happened, with a bonus for anything valuable they bring back.

### Scenes

- **The false light.** Night falls as the ship nears the reef, and the lighthouse blazes to life. The navigator must trust the stars over the light; following it runs the ship onto the rocks. Failure means a scraped hull and a rough landing, not disaster.
- **The ship graveyard.** A lagoon full of wrecks to search for loot and clues. A giant octopus lives in the biggest one. A young stowaway who survived a wreck is hiding here and tells the crew about the wreckers.
- **Wreckers' Cove.** The pirates' camp sits in sea caves below the cliffs, with sailors from the lost ships held for ransom. The crew can sneak in, bluff, or fight.
- **The lighthouse.** A spiral stair climbs the cliff tower. At the top, Raven Heartgrove and the rival are haggling over the caged firebird. Big swashbuckling fight on the stairs and in the lantern room: swinging from the great lamp's chains and knocking pirates off the gallery into the sea.
- **The choice.** The firebird can be freed or sold. The Guild would pay a fortune for it. Freed, it bursts into the sky, lights a safe path through the reef, and leaves the crew a feather that never stops glowing.
- **Escape.** With the cove in uproar, the crew gets the rescued sailors back to the ship while the rival slips away.

**The prize.** The first safe chart of the reef for the Guild, loot from the wrecks, grateful sailors with friends back in Kalatecha, and either a fortune or a firebird feather, depending on their choice.

## When they return to Kalatecha

### The widow on the pier

**What happened.** After the dock ambush in Session 1, Ander rolled the dead deckhands into the harbor and kept the 15 silver they carried. They were already dead when they went in. Each of them had been paid five silver to grab the lantern. The captain reported the fight to the City Guard. [[npc:ln_okoro|Sergeant Okoro]] wrote it down, but nobody really investigated, and nobody went looking for the dead men's families.

**The scene.** Run it the first time the crew steps onto the pier after they come back. Read:

> The pier is busy, but one woman isn't working. Her eyes are red and swollen, and she's been going from sailor to sailor. When she sees the Sallowmay, she pushes through the crowd, grabs a fistful of Ander's coat and holds on. "You were here. You were on this pier the day you sailed. My Tomas took a job on the docks that day. He said he'd be home by supper with five silver. He never came home." Her voice breaks. "Do you know what happened to him? Please. Anything."

**Who she is.** [[npc:ln_vela|Lucia Vela]], a net-mender from the harbor. Her husband, Tomas Vela, was one of the deckhands Ander rolled into the water. She doesn't know he was hired to steal anything. She only knows he went out for a day's work and never came back.

**The more they talk to her, the worse it gets.** Don't give it all at once. Each time they ask another question, stay with her, or offer help, she tells them a little more:

1. Tomas took any work he could find. Hauling, gutting fish, night watch on the warehouses. He'd stopped sleeping.
2. He sold his little fishing boat in the spring. They're behind on the rent, and the landlord has been knocking.
3. Their daughter, Sofia, is six. She's been sick since winter, and the healers at the cathedral can't fix it. She needs surgery from a surgeon in the Upper Town, who wants 10 gold up front before he'll touch her.
4. Every coin Tomas earned went into a jar under the floorboards for that fee. The day he disappeared, he told her he'd found "an easy job, five silver for an afternoon," and that it would almost fill the jar. . The landlord wants them out by the end of the month. Under Kalatecha's law, when a parent or a child dies, the family can't be evicted from their home for the year of mourning. But there's no body, so the landlord says Tomas just skipped town and left them, and they'll have to live with it. Lucia won't hear it. Tomas loved Sofia more than anything, and he would never have walked out on them. Something happened to him. She knows it.

So the man who attacked them wasn't a thug. He was a desperate father who took the wrong job. The five silver he was paid is in Ander's purse.

**The law of mourning (Kalatecha).** If a parent or a child dies, their family can't be evicted from their home for one year, the legal period of grieving. The death has to be shown: a body, or the sworn word of a witness before the Admiralty. That makes the crew's honesty worth something. Their word could keep Lucia and Sofia in their home.

**A way to make it right.** If the crew wants to help, the surgeon's fee is 10 gold. With the jar she's only about a gold short, but the landlord wants the rent first, and the rent would empty the jar. The crew can pay the whole fee, or just what she's short and the rent. If they pay it, Sofia gets her surgery, and Lucia never forgets the crew's faces, for better or worse depending on what they told her.

**How it can go.**

- **They tell her the truth.** She falls apart, then asks where he is so she can bury him. If they'll swear to what happened before [[npc:ln_quell|Commodore Quell]] at the Admiralty, the landlord can't put them out. The harbor has had all the time they were away to take the body. If they help her look, the twins [[npc:ln_twins|Nico and Lina]] know where the tide washes things up under the bridge.
- **They lie or say nothing.** She lets go and walks away, but she keeps asking around the docks. People saw the fight. Sooner or later, someone tells her which crew it was.
- **They give her money.** The 15 silver from the bodies was partly her husband's pay. If Ander gives her any of it, she knows the coins are from the docks and asks why he has them.

**The consequence.** This is a quiet consequence, not a punishment. Let it sting. [[npc:ln_hargrove|Hargrove-Vance]] keeps a journal of the crew's mistakes, and a grieving widow and a missing body are just what he wants. If the crew lies to her, he finds her before they sail again.
