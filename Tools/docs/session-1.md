# Session 1: Hired in Kalatecha

Session 1 stays in Kalatecha and runs about 2 hours 20 minutes plus a break. It ends with the ship leaving the harbor at dusk. Everything has to happen in order, but any scene can shrink if you're running long. The fight in Scene 5 is the one to cut first.

Tick each part as you finish it. The run sheet at the top keeps the time, and the 📍 beside a part shows its place on the map; while this tab is open, every scene's place is numbered on the map. Read-aloud text is in the shaded boxes. Paraphrase it freely.

If you're running long at the break, play Scene 5 as a quick chase (about 10 minutes) instead of a fight.

The crew: Xuna Kor'larym (Rebekah), navigator; Rin Amakiir (Elisabeth), medic; Ander (Benjamin), master-at-arms; Ethal Vaelor (Hannah), quartermaster.

## Opening {10 min} {place: kcity}

Put the city picture on the TV (top bar: **Players' screen** → Pictures → Show), or in the middle of the table, then read:

> The sun is warm on the red-tiled rooftops of Kalatecha, the free city by the sea. Tall-masted ships crowd the harbor, their sails patched with the colors of a dozen far-off ports. A stone fortress keeps watch over the coast. Above the market square, a great domed cathedral rises, and its bells ring out across the water.
> Kalatecha is where every voyage begins and ends. Sailors come home with strange spices, stranger stories, and maps of places no one has seen. Out past the horizon lie islands no one has charted: ruins, monsters, and peoples no one in Kalatecha has ever met.
> Each of you answered a notice on the longest pier, met its captain, and signed on. This morning, you report aboard.

Ask each player to point at one spot on the picture they'd like to visit someday. Remember their answers; they make good hooks for later sessions.

## Scene 1: Reporting aboard {30 min} {place: kcity/psallow}

The characters have already answered the captain's notice, and he's already hired them. This morning they report to the ship. Before he gives out the jobs, he asks each of them one last interview question; then he explains the four crew roles. Start by reading:

> The notice is still nailed to a post at the end of the longest pier. CREW WANTED. Honest hands for a voyage to uncharted waters. Ask aboard the Sallowmay. Since you last saw it, someone has written across it in neat, old-fashioned script: Positions filled.
> The Sallowmay sits at the end of the pier. She's not the biggest ship in the harbor, but she might be the best kept. Her deck is scrubbed white, her ropes are coiled neat as sleeping snakes, and every fitting gleams with polished bronze. A man leans on the rail at the top of the gangplank as if he's been waiting for you. On his shoulder sits a fairy no bigger than your hand, with dragonfly wings and a thimble for a helmet, looking you over as if you were fish at the market.

**Show the picture.** Put it on the TV: in the top bar, **Players' screen** → Pictures → The Sallowmay: hiring the crew. Two small clues are painted in: a sprig of hawthorn blossom growing out of the rail, and the silver markings on Thimble's arms. Don't point them out. If a player notices one, smile and move on.

**The captain greets them.** Read:

> "Right on time. Good. The sea doesn't wait, and neither do I." His eyes are the green of deep water, and his smile doesn't quite reach them. "You've all signed on, and I don't go back on a bargain. But before I tell you your jobs, I have one more question for each of you. Humor me."

**The last interview questions.** Go around the table. Each player gives:

- Their character's name and what they look like.
- Why they signed on. Are they after money, adventure, or getting away from something?

After each introduction, the captain asks that character one last question from the rail. Pick from these or make up your own:

- "What's the farthest you've ever been from home?"
- "Can you swim?"
- "What would you do if you found a treasure no one could ever trace back to you?"
- "Do you keep your promises?" (Note what they say. It matters for the favor track.)

Whatever they answer, he nods as if it's the answer he expected. Nobody fails this interview; he's learning who they are.

**The captain welcomes them.** Once everyone has answered:

> "Then welcome aboard. I'm Captain Callomay. The Sallowmay is mine, and she's the finest ship in Kalatecha, though I'm biased. You'll have a fair share of everything we earn, and you'll eat better than any crew in this harbor. In return, you sail her."
> "One thing you should know. I don't go ashore, and I don't go exploring. I stay with the ship. Out there, the decisions are yours."
> The fairy on his shoulder sniffs. "He means me too. I'm Thimble. Don't call me his familiar."

If anyone asks for his full name, he smiles and says, "Captain Callomay will do." If anyone asks why he never leaves the ship, he says, "Someone has to keep her safe." Don't explain further. His true name is Calandrous Callomaydare. Never say it at the table. If they ask about Thimble, he answers truthfully: "People call her my familiar. She calls herself my conscience."

**Crew roles.** All four roles are settled: Xuna (Rebekah) is the navigator, Rin (Elisabeth) the medic, Ander (Benjamin) the master-at-arms, and Ethal (Hannah) the quartermaster. They're already on the Party tab.

| Role | What they do | Good fit for |
| Medic | Keeps the crew alive and tends the sick and injured (Rin) | Clerics, druids, anyone good at Medicine |
| Navigator | Reads stars and charts and draws the crew's map (Xuna) | Anyone good at Survival or with a high Wisdom or Intelligence |
| Quartermaster | Handles supplies, money, and trading (Ethal) | Bards, rogues, anyone with a high Charisma |
| Master-at-arms | Leads in battle and keeps the ship's weapons (Ander) | Fighters, barbarians, paladins |

**The captain explains the roles.** Go around the table and read each part to the player who holds that role, handing over the prop as you go. From now on he calls each of them by their role ("Navigator," "Medic") until they earn their names. Start with:

> "A ship like this needs four people who know their jobs, and one who stays out of their way. That's me. So, listen."

**Navigator** (Xuna). Hand Rebekah the blank map with Kalatecha drawn in.

> "Navigator. You read the stars and the charts, and you tell her where to go. She'll listen to you, I promise. And you draw the map. Every coast we find goes on that page in your hand, not mine. I've never had the hand for it."

*DM only: the true reason is that fae ink won't hold a mortal coast. Don't explain it.*

**Medic** (Rin).

> "Medic. You keep this crew breathing. There's a healer's kit below, and it's yours now. Anyone who comes back hurt comes to you first, and that includes the ones who say they're fine."

**Quartermaster** (Ethal). Hand Hannah the ship's purse: **25 gold pieces, 33 silver and 65 copper.** It's the ship's money, not hers; it's written in her inventory in the app.

> "Quartermaster. The purse, the stores, and every bargain we strike. You'll know what we have, what we need, and what a thing is worth before anyone else on the dock does. Spend it well. Spend it, mind. A full purse never charted anything."

**The coins.** Nothing looks odd about them at a glance. If Ethal, or anyone, looks them over properly, they can make an Intelligence (Investigation) or Intelligence (History) check, DC 15. On a success: the coins were minted by three different countries, and only one of those countries still exists. The other two are gone, known now only from old maps and histories. (DM only: the sea pays him, out of wrecks from all over her waters and across three hundred years. No one ever sees him earn it.)

**Master-at-arms** (Ander).

> "Master-at-arms. When there's trouble, the crew looks to you. You keep the ship's weapons, and you decide when they come out. The locker's below." He hands over a small key. It's bronze, like every other fitting on the ship.

**Thimble** pipes up before anyone can ask:

> "And I'm the one who tells him when you're all doing it wrong."

**The captain finishes:**

> "Which leaves me. I keep the Sallowmay. I'll take you wherever you choose, and I'll be here when you come back. But out there, the choices are yours. All of them."

That last line is the bargain talking: only charts made by mortal choice count (see the Captain Callomay document). Keep it light, and let them decide everything.

**The ship.** The Sallowmay is her own place in the app. In DM mode, click her at the pier and press **Enter the Sallowmay**: inside are the [[place:kship/sdeck|Main deck]], the [[place:kship/squarters|Captain's quarters]] and [[place:kship/shold|Below decks]], and each one's **Enter this place** opens its map. Use them to show the crew around their new home, and for any fight aboard later. Anyone who searches the captain's quarters (they shouldn't, yet) can find the ship's log (DC 15) and, under the bunk, a sealed scallop shell (DC 20).

**Clue to drop.** When a player looks around the ship, describe the bronze: belaying pins, cleats, and the hilt of the captain's sword are all bronze. There isn't a scrap of iron anywhere on deck. Mention it once and move on.

**Send them ashore.**

> "We'll need supplies before we sail, and the Guild of Explorers has sent word that they have work for the Sallowmay. Go to the market, then to the Charter House. Bring back what we need, and bring me back whatever you hear."

**Favor watch.** This is the first chance to mark favor. Thanking him or complimenting the ship earns a +1 at the end of the night. Being rude to him earns a −1. Being rude to Thimble counts too; she tells him.

## Scene 2: The market {20 min} {place: kcity/ptobias}

The crew buys supplies and finds the lantern that burns without oil. Read:

> The market square is loud and bright. Stalls spill over with oranges, figs, and sacks of red and yellow spice. Gulls wheel overhead, waiting for someone to drop a fish. The cathedral's shadow falls across half the square, and in the cool of it, merchants sell things from far away: carved shells, feathered masks, a stuffed lizard as long as a rowboat.

**Supplies.** When the quartermaster tries to pay for food, rope, or water, every merchant waves the money away: "Already settled. Your captain's account." No one remembers him coming ashore to settle it. This is the second clue. Let it hang. (DM only: Thimble did it this morning, invisible. A player who makes a DC 15 Perception check spots a glint of dragonfly wings above the spice stall, gone in a blink.)

**The merchant.** At the edge of the square, in the cathedral's shadow, [[npc:ln_tobias|Old Tobias Pell]] sells salvage: coils of wet rope, a cracked compass, a figurehead with no face. He is a jumpy little man with a one-eyed gull named Admiral on his shoulder, and Admiral steals anything shiny. If anyone flashes a coin, Admiral grabs it and Tobias apologizes for him. Read:

> On a square of old sailcloth sits a brass lantern, its glass crusted with salt and tiny barnacles. Inside, a small flame burns a bright, steady gold. There's no wick and no oil. The flame just floats there, warm as a hand on your cheek.

Tobias wants 10 gold pieces for [[loot:ll_lantern|the lantern]] and won't say where it came from. He gets nervous if they press him. Let the players poke at it:

| Check | DC | What they learn |
| Insight | 10 | He keeps glancing toward the docks. He's scared of someone, not of the crew. |
| Investigation | 12 | The salt and barnacles mean it's been underwater for weeks. It came off a wreck. |
| Arcana | 12 | This isn't ordinary magic. The flame feels alive, and it always leans the same way, south, no matter how the lantern is turned. |
| Persuasion | 12 | He admits it came from a wreck on the southern reefs, sold to him by "people you don't want to meet." That's all he'll say. |

**Haggling.** A quartermaster who makes a DC 12 Persuasion check gets it for 5 gold pieces. If the crew is kind to Tobias and short of money, he'll let it go cheap just to be rid of it.

## Scene 3: The rival {15 min} {place: kcity/ptobias}

The rival explorer tries to take the lantern out from under the crew. Start just as the crew is about to buy it. Read:

> The crowd parts for a lean, silver-haired man in a fine Guild coat with the badges picked off. A monocle glints in one eye, a leather journal is tucked under his arm, and a swirl of silver just shows above his collar. Two broad-shouldered deckhands follow close behind. "Well, well." Rupert Hargrove-Vance doesn't look at you, only at the lantern. "Merchant, I'll give you twenty gold for that, and you can forget you ever had it."

**What [[npc:ln_hargrove|Hargrove-Vance]] does.**

- Offers double whatever the crew offers, up to 30 gold pieces.
- Talks down to the crew: "A ship full of new hires? How sweet. Does your captain let you hold the wheel?"
- Boasts about sailing first: "The Undaunted sails at dawn. Wherever that trinket came from, I'll be there before you've finished packing."
- Opens his journal and reads aloud: "Day one. New crew of the Sallowmay. Already overpaying for junk." He writes down every mistake they make, and says it out loud as he writes.

**His magic, just a glimpse.** He's a real warlock, and he doesn't want anyone to know it here. Give the table one odd detail and nothing more: the pages of his journal turn by themselves in a wind no one else feels, or Admiral shrieks and won't go near him. A player who makes a DC 15 Arcana check feels fae magic on him, cold and sharp, nothing like the captain's. (DM only: his familiar, Nib, a spirit in a sprite's shape, rides invisible on his collar. If Thimble is with the crew, she hisses at the empty air there.)

**Who gets the lantern.** Tobias sells to whoever treated him better, and the rival is rude to him. If the crew has been decent at all, he sells to them. If the rival does end up with it, the deckhands are carrying it in Scene 5, and the fight becomes about getting it back.

**As the rival leaves,** describe a look between the rival and the two deckhands. A player who makes a DC 12 Insight check catches it: the rival isn't done.

**Why the rival wants it (DM only).** He buys loot from the wreckers on Lantern Rock. This lantern slipped through and ended up in the market, and he wants it back before anyone traces it to the reef. Since the Guild stopped favoring him, that loot is how he pays for the Undaunted. There's more: his patron, Vesperine of the Thorn, an Unseelie archfey and the captain's old rival at court, wants Captain Callomay to lose his wager, so the Sallowmay's crew must never finish a chart. Stopping this crew is his real job. The players can piece this together over many sessions.

## Snack break {15 min}

Good stopping point: the crew has the lantern and knows it has a rival.

## Scene 4: The Charter House {15 min} {place: kcity/pcharter}

[[npc:ln_fontaine|Michael Fontaine]] hires the ship to chart Lantern Rock. Read:

> The Charter House of the Guild of Explorers is a grand building of pale stone with tall arched windows. Inside, every wall is covered in maps, and half of every map is empty. In the middle of the hall stands a huge globe. The parts no one has explored are painted with sea serpents.
> A tall, blond man in his early thirties hurries over. He's holding a stack of papers and has ink on his fingers. "You must be the new crew of the Sallowmay. Michael Fontaine, Guild of Explorers. Your captain's letter came this morning, so he's already said yes. Come, come."

**Another odd detail.** If anyone asks who delivered the captain's letter, Fontaine frowns: "It was on my desk when I came in. I assumed one of the clerks…" (DM only: Thimble, through the window. There's a tiny wet footprint on the sill, smaller than a mouse's.)

**When he sees the lantern,** he stops cold and lowers his voice:

> "Where did you get that? No, never mind. That's exactly why I asked for you." He unrolls a chart. Far to the south, a small island is circled in red. "Lantern Rock. There's a lighthouse on it that has shone every night for as long as anyone remembers, and no one lives there. Ships that follow its light don't come back. We've lost three this year."

**The commission.** Hand over the sealed letter prop. Then, in the [[place:kcity/pcharter|Charter House]]'s panel (DM mode), set the Lantern Rock mission on its board to **Taken**: that puts [[place:klantern|Lantern Rock]] on the sea chart as a known route, so the players see the way there. Un-hide the reef around it too, in its own panel. If you like, show them the board on the TV first ("Show it on the players' screen") with the job still marked Posted. The Guild offers:

- 50 gold pieces each to chart a safe channel through the reef and find out what happened to the lost ships.
- A bonus for anything valuable they bring back.
- A copy of the Guild's chart. Have Rebekah draw Lantern Rock onto the crew's map, about two days' sail south. The reef around it stays blank.

A quartermaster who makes a DC 15 Persuasion check raises the fee to 60 gold pieces each.

**If they ask Fontaine questions:**

- **Why us?** "Nobody else will take it. Well, Hargrove-Vance has been asking about Lantern Rock all week, which worries me."
- **Who is Hargrove-Vance?** "He was the Guild's best explorer, once. He thinks we owe him every job worth having. We don't."
- **Has the Guild sent anyone?** "One ship, two months ago, the Heron. She never came back."
- **Is it magic?** "I sit behind a desk now. Ask me about paperwork."
- **Why did he stop adventuring?** "I used to do this, you know. Then I discovered chairs."

## Scene 5: Trouble on the docks {25 min} {place: kcity/pambush}

Hargrove-Vance's deckhands try to take the lantern as the crew heads back to the ship. Read:

> The docks are busy with the end of the day's work. Cranes swing cargo nets overhead, crates are stacked higher than your heads, and the Sallowmay is just in sight at the end of the pier. Then three figures step out from behind the crates ahead, and three more behind you. You recognize the rival's deckhands. "We'll take that lantern," says one. "No need for anyone to get wet."

**The fight.** Use the [[place:kcity/pambush|Dock Ambush]] in the app: six of [[foe:lf_deckhand|Hargrove-Vance's deckhands]], built on the SRD Tough stat block. Its battle map is the docks: the pier to the Sallowmay, the crane with its rope and cargo net, two stacks of crates where the deckhands hide, a fish cart, and the warehouses behind. Everyone starts in place: the crew on the quay, three deckhands at the crates ahead and three at the crates behind. That's a Low fight for four level-3 characters under the 2024 rules: a warm-up. Keep it fast:

- The deckhands want the lantern, not a killing. If a character drops to 0 hit points, the deckhands grab the lantern and run instead of finishing them.
- When two deckhands are down, or when the crew has the lantern safe, the rest flee. Mark them Fled in the app.
- Let the master-at-arms decide whether to chase them.

**Things to use on the docks.** Reward players who use the scenery:

| Action | Check | Result |
| Swing on a crane's rope | Athletics or Acrobatics, DC 10 | Land anywhere on the dock, and get advantage on the next attack |
| Topple a stack of crates | Athletics, DC 12 | Everyone under it is knocked prone |
| Shove someone off the pier | Athletics against their Athletics or Acrobatics | They're in the water and out of the fight for a round |
| Drop a cargo net | Dexterity, DC 12 | One deckhand is tangled and can't move until they get free |

**Quick-chase version (10 minutes).** If time is short, skip the fight. One deckhand snatches the lantern and runs. The crew needs two successes out of three:

- Leap a fish cart: Athletics, DC 12.
- Cross a narrow gangplank: Acrobatics, DC 12.
- Spot the deckhand in the crowd: Perception, DC 12.

If they fail, the deckhand drops the lantern off the pier as he escapes. Someone has to dive in after it: Athletics, DC 10. Underwater, the lantern is still burning.

**Afterward.** Give the medic a chance to patch up the crew. Anyone who searches a fallen deckhand rolls Wisdom (Perception) in the app: each one carries the five silver he was paid, and one has [[loot:ll_hvnote|a note signed H.V.]] in his pocket (DC 10). A captured deckhand talks if treated well: "Hargrove-Vance paid us five silver each. Said it wasn't worth the trouble anyway."

## Scene 6: Set sail {10 min} {place: kcity/psallow}

The crew returns to the ship, and the Sallowmay leaves the harbor at dusk. The captain is at the rail when they come back. He asks what happened, and he listens closely if they mention the rival. Telling him the truth earns favor. Thimble wants every detail of the fight, and takes the side of whoever told it best.

Go around the table and ask each player for one thing their character does as the ship casts off. Some ideas by role:

- **Navigator:** Xuna sets the course south and pins the map to the chart table.
- **Quartermaster:** Ethal stows the supplies and counts the purse.
- **Medic:** Rin sets up a sick bay below deck.
- **Master-at-arms:** Ander checks the ship's weapons and keeps an eye on the Undaunted.

Then read:

> The sails fill and the Sallowmay slides away from the pier. Behind you, Kalatecha glows gold in the setting sun. Then the cathedral bells begin to ring. People on the docks stop and look up. It's not the evening hour, and it's not a pattern anyone in the city has heard before.
> At the rail, Captain Callomay closes his eyes and smiles as if the bells were meant for him.
> On the chart table, the lantern's little flame leans south, toward a lighthouse no one tends and a reef no one has crossed. Lantern Rock is waiting.

End the session there.

**The ship's log.** Ask who wants to keep the ship's log. That player writes a few lines tonight or before next session, and you'll read it aloud to open Session 2. In the app it goes in the Notes tab's Ship's log; "Draft from this session" gives them a start from tonight's game log.

### Quick rules reference

| Situation | Rule |
| How hard is a check? | Easy DC 10, medium DC 15, hard DC 20 |
| Something helps or hurts | Advantage: roll two d20s and take the higher. Disadvantage: take the lower. |
| A character drops to 0 hit points | Death saving throws each turn: a d20 roll of 10 or higher is a success. Three successes and they're stable; three failures and they die. |
| A natural 20 on a death save | They regain 1 hit point |
| A natural 1 on a death save | Counts as two failures |
| A character dies | The captain's safety net: see "The rescue and the reveal" in the Captain Callomay document |
